# Radar Signal Selection Based on Contrastive Time-frequency Learning [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Radar reconnaissance is an integral part of modern electronic countermeasures technology and the cornerstone of electronic defense. The primary phase in radar signal discrimination is signal classification. With the advancement of radar technology, the complexity of the electromagnetic environment continues to expand [1]. Effectively and efficiently classifying signals is a critical issue.
Traditional radar signal classification algorithms typically use pulse-Doppler (PDW), which includes carrier frequency (CF), pulse width (PW), pulse amplitude (PA), arrival time (TOA), and direction of arrival (DOA) [3]. In addition to interpulse features, intrapulse modulation features are also important manifestations of subtle characteristics of radar signals [4]. Using intrapulse features for signal classification and recognition has become an important research technique. However, single-domain or domain analysis methods cannot well describe the changing patterns and characteristics of signals. Time-frequency analysis can better reflect the energy density distribution of non-stationary signals. By mapping one-dimensional signals to two-dimensional time-frequency domains, time-frequency analysis can greatly improve the robustness of algorithms and reduce the impact of noise.
An important step in signal classification is feature extraction. Deep learning has demonstrated impressive capabilities in computer vision and natural language processing fields in terms of feature extraction. Many studies have been conducted using deep learning knowledge such as reinforcement learning and transfer learning to autonomously extract features from radar signals. The application of deep learning theory in radar signal processing is already one of the most promising research areas [6].
Deep learning models' performance is often closely related to the quality of the dataset. Robust deep learning networks with good generalization capabilities are typically obtained through training on large labeled data sets. However, building a large radar dataset containing annotated labels is very challenging due to the increasing number of unknown radars in electronic countermeasures. Unsupervised data is easier to obtain than supervised data. To reduce dependence on labeled data, unsupervised data can be used to train networks for signal classification. Therefore, it is worth exploring methods to mine unsupervised data features using heuristic tasks, such as predicting image rotation angles or puzzle-solving tasks [7]. For radar signal segregation, a suitable segregation task needs to match the characteristics of radar signals.
This paper proposes a radar signal segregation method based on time-frequency analysis and contrastive learning—Contrastive Time-Frequency Learning (CTFL). The goal of this method is to reduce dependence on labeled data and enhance the generalization capability of radar signal segregation methods. Based on Multi-Synchronous Compression Transform (MSST), the signal is transformed into a two-dimensional time-frequency image. The enhanced time-frequency image (TFI) is used to train a contrastive learning model to extract features from the signal. With the characteristics of radar signals, effective classification can be achieved.

Experimental results will be presented in Section 4, and the results of the proposed method will be compared with several reference methods. Section 5 concludes the paper.
Related work has been proposed for radar signal separation based on time-frequency analysis and machine learning. Choi-Williams distribution (CWD) is utilized for time-frequency analysis, followed by filtering of the signals' potential features using information theory [8]. A multilayer perceptron is employed to classify radar signals with a signal-to-noise ratio of 6dB. To enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of the separation system, transfer learning is applied to the selection of intra-pulse features of radar signals. Transfer learning is used to reconstruct the energy cube, which represents the original radar signals [9]. Support vector machines are then used for feature classification, improving both accuracy and robustness. All these feature extraction methods require extensive expertise from professionals.The feature extraction method based on experience is clear in meaning. However, its application is limited by the type of signal. These features are not very general. Therefore, the classification of new modulated signals is generally unreliable. Furthermore, Overfitting is also an issue that cannot be ignored. When the dimensionality of features increases, Accuracy cannot be improved accordingly.
To make the classification method universal, automatic feature extraction is required. In recent years, deep learning theory has developed rapidly and demonstrated excellent performance and strong generalization capabilities in fields such as computer vision and natural language processing. Deep learning brings many possibilities and innovations for automatically extracting features from signals. The introduction of Cohen class time-frequency distribution kernel functions improves radar signal sorting [10]. It performs well for sorting signals with low signal-to-noise ratio. Besides CNN, random forest (RFF) is also used for sorting radar signals. Stacked Sparse Autoencoder (SSAE) is used to identify low intercept probability radar signals [11]. CWD is used for time-frequency transformation, then RFF is used to identify the features extracted by SSAE. The accuracy of this method can reach 96.4% [12]. However, all these methods require a large training set to achieve high accuracy.
This paper proposes a radar signal sorting method based on deep learning that can utilize unsupervised data instead of supervised data. Compared with existing methods, this method has better generalization capability and its feature representation module can effectively improve the accuracy of classification tasks under low signal-to-noise ratio.
Comparative Time-Frequency Learning (Ctfl) is proposed for sorting radar signals. Firstly, the received signals are transformed into time-frequency images. Secondly, contrast learning is used to extract unlabeled features using TFIS. Finally, the extracted features are used to classify the signals.

Inertial modulation features can be considered as manifestations of radar hardware characteristics, also known as radio frequency fingerprints. Intentional modulation features are generated by amplitude modulation, frequency modulation or phase modulation. This paper considers both intentional and inertial modulation features without requiring them to be separated.

For image data, convolutional neural networks are commonly used for automatic feature extraction methods. Radar signals are mostly unannotated unsupervised data that cannot be directly utilized by traditional deep convolutional neural networks. Therefore, feature extraction methods based on unsupervised data are particularly important. The labels need to be extracted from the data itself, which usually requires specific tasks to construct pseudo supervision information. Since TFI does not have a wide range of continuous features, we use contrastive learning to build a radar signal feature extraction module.

Researchers have applied contrastive learning to many well-known methods, such as MoCo [15], Simplified [16], SwAV [17], BYOL [18]. Contrastive learning can be used to train the encoder network with positive and negative pairs. With this encoder network, instances can be mapped into the feature space where similar instances are closer and dissimilar ones are farther apart. Time-frequency images contain rich features, and contrastive learning can effectively learn high-dimensional features of signals.
A typical contrastive learning process is shown in Figure 2. The positive and negative samples are represented as v+ and v-, respectively. The positive samples are enhanced images, while the negative samples are other radars' TFIs. The encoder is denoted by 𝑓(.) and the projector by g(.). By optimizing the loss function, the distance between the positive samples can be sufficiently small, and their distance from the negative samples can be sufficiently large, as shown in Equation (8).
Contrastive learning extracts semantic features of the input image by separating the positive and negative samples. Negative samples are unnecessary. SwAV [17], BYOL [18], and SimSiam [19] do not involve negative samples but can still achieve competitive performance using only positive samples. Since the TFIs of different radars are very similar, there are no obvious negative samples in the TFIs. If we force to distinguish between positive and negative samples, network training will not converge. Therefore, we use unbiased contrastive learning to extract TFI features without negative samples.
Moreover, the TFIs of radar signals do not have continuous sawtooth target boundaries, and the background color changes are not abrupt.
